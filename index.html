Scots' plates fail every healthy-eating test

Too much fat, sugar and salt, not enough fibre: what official figures reveal about what we really eat.

Annah Chantemesse Health reporter

17

sugar cubes. Every day.

The average Scot takes home 68 g of added sugar a day in their shopping. That's 17 sugar cubes, every single day.

Each cube falling at the top of this page stands for 4 g of that sugar.

And sugar is only the start. Official figures from the UK Government's Family Food survey show Scottish households buy far more saturated fat and salt than health experts recommend.

At the same time, we're nowhere near eating enough fibre.

The Daily Record put the Scottish plate through four simple tests, based on the Scottish Dietary Goals: the official healthy-eating targets used by the Scottish Government and Food Standards Scotland.

It failed three of them and missed the fourth.

How to read it

Each bar shows how much we buy compared with the official target. The dotted line marks 100%: the target itself.

For saturated fat, salt and sugar, a bar above the line means a limit has been broken. For fibre, the bar stops short of the line: that's a goal we're missing.

The red bars show the food we buy for home. Press "Add eating out" to put a dark blue bar next to each one: the same score once meals eaten outside the home are included.

Hover over a bar, or tap it on your phone, to see the score and the real amount we buy. Click it to see which foods it comes from. You can also click the labels below the chart, or press "Hide target line" to see the bars on their own.

Test 1: saturated fat

135%

Saturated fat makes up 14.9% of the calories in the food we buy. The Scottish Dietary Goals say it should be no more than 11%.

That's 135% of the limit, the worst result on the plate.

Food Standards Scotland warns that eating too much fat, especially saturated fat, can increase the risk of heart disease. It hides in butter, cheese, fatty meat, pies, pastries and cakes.

Test 2: salt

118%

The average Scot buys food containing 7.1 g of salt a day. The limit is 6 g, about a teaspoon.

That's 118% of the limit. And the real figure is even higher, because the survey doesn't count the salt we shake on at the table.

Most of it is already in our food before we open the packet. According to Food Standards Scotland, around 75% of the salt we eat comes from packaged foods like bread, breakfast cereal and ready meals.

Too much salt raises blood pressure and the risk of heart disease and stroke.

Test 3: sugar

109%

Added sugar provides 12% of our calories, just over the 11% limit used in the survey.

It may look like a narrow fail, but it adds up to those 17 sugar cubes a day.

And today's advice is much stricter. Food Standards Scotland says adults should have no more than 30 g of free sugars a day: around 7 sugar cubes. The Scottish Dietary Goals now put the limit at 5% of our energy.

Test 4: fibre

61%

Fibre is the one thing we should eat more of, and we're falling well short.

Food Standards Scotland says adults should aim for 30 g of fibre a day. The survey uses an older way of measuring fibre, in which that target works out at about 23 g.

We get just 14 g a day from the food we buy. That's only six grams in every ten we need.

Fibre is linked to a lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer, and helps us feel fuller for longer.

What about meals out?

Food eaten outside the home, from chippy teas to meal deals, makes up only about 1 in 10 of our calories.

Press "Add eating out" on the chart to see the difference. Salt jumps from 107% to 118% of the limit, and fibre edges up from 55% to 61%.

But the fat and sugar bars barely move. Most of the problem starts in our own shopping trolleys.

Why it matters

Food Standards Scotland says that in Scotland "we're generally eating too much fat", and that most of us don't eat enough fibre.

Carrying excess weight raises our chances of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers.

The good news? Small swaps in the weekly shop can make a real difference.

What can you do?

  • Check food labels and choose the lower-fat and lower-salt options.
  • Have sweets, cakes and sugary drinks less often, and keep fruit juice to one small glass (150 ml) a day.
  • Swap white bread for wholemeal, and try porridge oats for breakfast.
  • Aim for at least five portions of fruit and veg a day.

More tips on the Food Standards Scotland website.

Your questions answered

How did we work out the scores?

We divided what the average Scot buys by the official target, then multiplied by 100.

For salt: 7.1 g a day ÷ 6 g × 100 = 118%. Anything over 100% means the limit has been broken.

Is this really what Scots eat?

Not exactly. The survey records the food and drink households say they bought. Some of it is wasted, and people tend to forget a few items.

Salt added at the table is not counted, so the real salt figure is even higher.

Why does sugar look different from today's advice?

The survey measures sugar as "non-milk extrinsic sugars" and fibre as "non-starch polysaccharides". We compared each one with a target in the same unit, so the scores are fair.

Is the data up to date?

The figures date from 2018/19, before COVID and the cost-of-living crisis, which have changed how many families shop.

Where do the figures come from?

Defra, Family Food datasets, Scottish Dietary Goals (2016) and Food Standards Scotland.
